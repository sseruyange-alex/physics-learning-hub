Experiment 1: Ohm's Law

Animated circuit simulation — V = IR

Aim

To verify that current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it at constant temperature (Ohm's Law).

Procedure

1
Set resistance to 100 Ω and increase voltage. Watch particle speed increase.
2
Fix voltage and increase resistance — watch particles slow down.
3
Read current from the green readout — this is V ÷ R (Ohm's Law).
4
Use Record to save readings and plot a graph to confirm the linear relationship.
The graph of Voltage vs Current should be a straight line through the origin, confirming V is proportional to I.

Experiment 2: Simple Pendulum

Measure g using period formula: T = 2π√(L/g)

Aim

To determine the acceleration due to gravity (g) by measuring the period of oscillation of a simple pendulum at different lengths.

Procedure

1
Set pendulum length using the slider.
2
Click Start to begin swinging; Stop to pause.
3
Time 20 complete swings, divide by 20 to find T.
4
Enter measured T to compute g = 4π²L / T².

Precaution: Keep the angle of release under 15° so the small-angle approximation holds and the formula T = 2π√(L/g) remains valid.

Experiment 3: Waves & Speed of Sound

Visualise wave motion and calculate wave speed: v = f × λ

Aim

To observe transverse wave motion and verify the wave speed equation v = fλ. To estimate the speed of sound using the echo method.

Wave Simulation
Frequency
2.0 Hz
Wavelength
170.0 m
Speed
340 m/s
Speed of Sound — Echo Method

Procedure

1
Stand at a measured distance d (50 m or more) from a large flat wall or cliff face.
2
Clap loudly. Listen for the echo. Time 20 claps and divide to get the time t for one echo.
3
Calculate: v = 2d / t. Sound travels to the wall and back.
4
Compare your answer to the accepted value of ~340 m/s at 20°C.
